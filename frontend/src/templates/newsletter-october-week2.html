📰 Cybersecurity News of the Week

Headline: 80% of Financial Institutions Struggle to Contain Cyber Threats - Report

Summary: About 80 per cent of financial institutions struggle to contain cyber threats automatically, as network disruptions increasingly affect customer transactions, digital banking and internal operations, a global survey of industry executives has found. According to the report by Nile, a provider of secure Network-as-a-Service, only 20.5 per cent of organisations automatically detect and quarantine threats in real time, while the remaining 79.5 per cent rely on manual intervention and struggle with limited visibility or delayed detection. The survey gathered responses from 322 IT, security and risk professionals across banking, insurance, payments, capital markets and fintech.

Source: Built In

📌 Key Details:

  • Impact: Network reliability emerged as another pressure point, with 57.1 per cent of respondents reporting disruptions monthly or more frequently, affecting customer transactions, trading activity, digital banking access and internal operations. Cybersecurity and regulatory risk were the leading ongoing network challenges at 24.2 per cent, followed by rising operational costs at 22.4 per cent. When asked about redesigning their networks, lowering total cost of ownership accounted for 25.2 per cent of responses, making it the top priority ahead of security. Compliance also remains heavily dependent on manual work: 59.3 per cent reported a moderate to significant manual burden in maintaining audit readiness, while only 19 per cent use automated reporting and policy enforcement. Only 18.6 per cent fully enforce Zero Trust across their networks, with legacy-system complexity identified as the leading barrier.
  • Affected: Financial institutions across banking, insurance, payments, capital markets and fintech. Third-party access remains a vulnerability: only 21 per cent of respondents said their organisations fully segment third-party access with enforced policy controls, meaning nearly four in five fell short of that standard. The report's findings have relevance for Nigeria, where banks, fintech companies and payment operators increasingly depend on interconnected digital infrastructure.
  • Mitigation: Nile's Chief Marketing Officer, Shashi Kiran, said financial institutions understand the need to modernise, but the complexity of legacy architectures makes it difficult to strengthen security without adding cost and manual effort. About 46.6 per cent of respondents already deploy AI-driven tools or plan to adopt them within the next 12 to 18 months, and 72.4 per cent said they would consider a secure network-as-a-service model. In Nigeria, the Central Bank of Nigeria has separately directed payment terminal operators and processors to establish dual connectivity to NIBSS and Unified Payment Services, seeking to reduce service interruptions associated with reliance on a single transaction route.

⚠️ Why It Matters: The research showed wide variations in network maturity among the institutions surveyed. Only about one in five reported advanced capabilities in individual areas, including automated threat containment, Zero Trust, audit reporting, third-party segmentation and continuous visibility, and just 1.9 per cent demonstrated advanced capabilities across five or more of the seven measures examined. For Nigeria, NIBSS data show digital-payment fraud losses of N25.85bn in 2025, a 51 per cent decline from N52.26bn in 2024, although fraud remained a significant risk across the country's rapidly expanding digital payments ecosystem. Reported fraud incidents also fell to 67,518 in 2025 from 123,918 in 2021. In an October 2026 advisory, ngCERT identified financial services among sectors exposed to the threat landscape, citing weaknesses including poor identity and access management, inadequate multi-factor authentication, unpatched systems and insufficient staff awareness. ngCERT has also warned of continuing threats including phishing, ransomware, business email compromise and data breaches.

🎯 Threat of the Week (Sector-by-Sector)

  • Enterprise Infrastructure & Networking: The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) has added a critical vulnerability affecting Citrix NetScaler products to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) Catalog, following confirmed evidence of active exploitation in the wild. The flaw — tracked as CVE-2026-88779 (Citrix NetScaler Improper Restriction of Operations within the Bounds of a Memory Buffer Vulnerability) — enables malicious actors to target internet-facing application delivery controllers and gateway appliances. By sending specially crafted network requests, attackers can trigger memory buffer errors to execute arbitrary code, bypass authentication boundaries, or destabilize critical enterprise gateway services.
    Read full analysis →

⚠️ Why It Matters: Edge networking devices like Citrix NetScaler serve as the primary gateway into corporate internal networks for remote workers and branch offices. Vulnerabilities in these perimeter assets are prime targets for threat actors because successful exploitation grants direct access behind the firewall without triggering traditional endpoint detection. Organizations relying on these devices must prioritize immediate patch deployment and audit access logs for signs of anomalous external memory access.

🛠️ Tool of the Week

Spotlight: Dangerzone (Safe Document Converter) — Free and open source

What it does:
Dangerzone takes potentially dangerous PDFs, office documents, or images and converts them to safe PDFs. You give it a document you don't know if you can trust, such as an email attachment. Inside a sandbox, it converts the document to a PDF (if it isn't already one), and then converts the PDF into raw pixel data. In a separate sandbox, it takes this pixel data and converts it back into a PDF. The sandbox has no network access, and Dangerzone can optionally OCR the safe PDF so it has a searchable text layer again.

Who should use it:

  • Journalists and newsroom staff who receive documents from sources
  • Anyone who receives email attachments from unknown or unverified senders
  • Anyone who downloads documents from the internet
  • Anyone who needs to open untrusted PDFs, Office documents or images

Why it matters:
PDFs and office documents are very complex files, and malicious documents are designed to target a specific piece of software, such as a PDF reader. Because Dangerzone rebuilds the document from pixels instead of opening the original, even a document that would normally hack your computer is converted into a safe PDF you can read. It works on Windows, Mac and Linux, supports more than 20 file types, and is maintained by Freedom of the Press Foundation.

⚔️ Attack Scenario (Case Study)

Incident: Adversary-in-the-Middle (AiTM) Reverse-Proxy Phishing & Session Hijacking

Entry Point (How it started):
Adversaries deployed a reverse-proxy phishing framework (such as Evilginx) hosted on a typo-squatted lookalike domain. Senior accounting staff received urgent emails that appeared to originate from a primary supplier's billing portal requesting confirmation of updated electronic invoices.

What the attacker did next:

  • The staff member clicked the link and was routed to the proxy server, which proxied requests to the organization's genuine Single Sign-On (SSO) login page in real time.
  • The user entered their valid corporate username and password.
  • The reverse proxy forwarded these credentials to the actual corporate identity provider, which sent an authentication prompt to the user's mobile authenticator app.
  • The victim approved the prompt; the genuine identity provider authenticated the login and issued an active session authentication cookie.
  • The reverse proxy intercepted and captured the authenticated session cookie mid-flight before passing the user to the genuine dashboard.
  • Attackers injected the stolen session cookie into their own browsers, completely bypassing the MFA requirement and gaining full corporate portal access without ever needing the victim's physical device or OTP code.
  • Once inside, the threat actors created unauthorized mail-forwarding inbox rules and modified downstream automated payment routing entries.

Final Impact:

  • Corporate Single Sign-On sessions were completely compromised despite active MFA protections.
  • Intercepted session tokens granted unauthorized access to internal enterprise resource planning (ERP) systems and corporate communication channels.
  • The incident demanded emergency token revocations across all identity providers, global session terminations, and an extensive audit of downstream financial pipelines.

🔑 Key Lesson: Standard multi-factor authentication (such as SMS codes, email OTPs, and mobile push prompts) does not protect against proxy-based session token theft. Organizations must transition to FIDO2/WebAuthn-based phishing-resistant MFA (such as hardware security keys or passkeys) and implement conditional access policies that continuously bind session cookies to device compliance signals.

💼 Career Spotlight

Cyber Threat Intelligence (CTI) Analyst

A Cyber Threat Intelligence (CTI) Analyst is responsible for collecting, evaluating, and analyzing information about cyber adversaries, their capabilities, infrastructure, and motives. Rather than merely reacting to firewall or antivirus alarms, a CTI analyst monitors dark web forums, technical telemetry, and open-source intelligence (OSINT) to decode adversary Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures (TTPs) mapped to frameworks like MITRE ATT&CK. They translate raw technical threat data into strategic advisories for executive boards and actionable detection rules (such as YARA and Sigma rules) for Security Operations Centers (SOC).

  • Salary: ₦550,000 - ₦7,400,000 /year (Estimated for Nigeria; specialized regional and remote intelligence positions offer higher compensation tiers)
  • Certs needed: Certified Threat Intelligence Analyst (C|TIA), GIAC Cyber Threat Intelligence (GCTI), Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP), CompTIA Security+, and proficiency with Threat Intelligence Platforms (TIPs) like MISP and OpenCTI.

📖 Cybersecurity Glossary

Data Exfiltration: The unauthorised transfer or theft of data from an organisation's systems to a location controlled by an attacker. In ransomware campaigns such as the XP95 activity described by NCC-CSIRT, stolen data is used for extortion, with threats of public release unless ransom demands are met.

Adversary-in-the-Middle (AiTM) Phishing: A phishing technique in which the attacker places a proxy server between the victim and the genuine login page, so the victim signs in to the real service through the attacker's server. The attacker can capture the session cookie issued after login and reuse it to access the account, even though the victim completed MFA. Phishing-resistant MFA such as FIDO2/WebAuthn is designed to resist this.
